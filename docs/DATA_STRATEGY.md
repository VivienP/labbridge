# LabBridge — data strategy

**Status:** planned  
**Scope:** observed HER replay data, synthetic biosensor data, provenance, redistribution, and scientific claim boundaries

LabBridge uses two environments behind one runtime adapter protocol:

1. an **Au–Ir–Rh HER replay environment** returning historical observed measurements;
2. an **enzymatic-biosensor simulation environment** generating explicitly synthetic signals.

They test the same infrastructure but they are not two fidelities of one scientific problem. Every campaign belongs to exactly one environment, and every record carries both its data origin and execution mode.

---

## 1. Classification model

| Environment | Data origin | Execution mode | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Au–Ir–Rh HER | `observed` | `replay` | Historical measurements originally produced by physical experiments, returned by a replay adapter |
| Au–Ir–Rh HER on the generated fixture | `synthetic` | `replay` | Independently generated, schema-compatible files returned by the same replay adapter (ADR-010) |
| Enzymatic biosensor | `synthetic` | `simulation` | Signals generated by a transparent, seeded phenomenological model |
| Future instrument adapter | `observed` | `live` | Reserved for an actual instrument integration; outside V1 |

The two fields are independent: origin says where the values came from, mode says how they reached the runtime. `observed` + `simulation` is inadmissible — simulation cannot produce observed data — and MUST be rejected by validation rather than treated as a configuration choice.

Human-readable reports MUST use the same distinctions. “Real” may describe the original HER data source, but a LabBridge replay run MUST NOT be called a live experiment or a physical-lab execution, and a fixture-backed run MUST NOT be called a replay of measured data.

## 2. Observed environment — Au–Ir–Rh HER replay

### 2.1 Pinned sources

**Dataset**  
Felix Thelen, Moonjoo Kim, Geovane Arruda de Oliveira, Jan Lukas Bürgel, Wolfgang Schuhmann, and Alfred Ludwig. *Dataset — Autonomous scanning electrochemical cell microscopy enables rapid exploration of large compositionally complex material spaces*. Zenodo, version 1, 28 May 2026. DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.20439519](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20439519).

**Associated preprint**  
Felix Thelen et al. *Autonomous scanning electrochemical cell microscopy enables rapid exploration of large compositionally complex material spaces*. arXiv:2606.00779, submitted 30 May 2026. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00779).

The dataset record is the authoritative source for archive contents. The paper is the authoritative source for scientific method and interpretation.

### 2.2 Verified high-level contents

The published record describes:

- three Au–Ir–Rh thin-film materials libraries, referred to as Au-rich, Ir-rich, and Rh-rich;
- a shared grid of 342 measurement areas per library;
- 20 areas per library excluded from SECCM because of collision risk;
- 322 LSV locations per library and 966 measured LSV locations overall;
- LSV files containing potential versus RHE, mean current density, and current-density standard deviation computed from three individually recorded LSVs;
- source-provided fitted parameters including limiting current density, transfer coefficient, and standard rate constant;
- EDX volume compositions for all grid areas;
- 13 measured XPS locations per library and GP-predicted XPS compositions for the full grid;
- XRD data for all grid areas;
- selected AFM raw data;
- active-learning animations and a measurement video.

LabBridge MUST preserve the distinction between:

- measured XPS values;
- GP-predicted XPS values;
- observed LSV data;
- source-provided fitted electrochemical parameters;
- any LabBridge-derived metric.

No predicted composition may be labelled as measured.

### 2.3 Gate 0 — source, licence, and schema verification

Before committing any archive-derived row, transformed sample, plot, or fixture, the implementation MUST:

1. confirm the applicable dataset licence or obtain explicit permission;
2. record the licence text and verification date;
3. determine whether redistribution of raw, transformed, and small fixture subsets is permitted;
4. record the decision in `docs/ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS.md` or a dedicated data-use decision.

**Status: satisfied by ADR-009.** The record declares `cc-by-4.0` with open access, read from the Zenodo REST API on 2026-07-30. Redistribution is permitted with attribution and an indication of changes.

The requirement that no parser close this gate stands regardless of the outcome. A licence string on a record is evidence, not a decision: parsing MUST yield `unresolved`, and only a recorded, dated decision pinned to the DOI and to that licence identifier may widen it. A record that stops declaring the licence the decision was verified against reopens the gate automatically.

Independently of the licence, and as a repository-hygiene policy rather than a restriction:

- the full archive remains user-fetched and git-ignored;
- offline tests use a schema-compatible synthetic fixture created independently of archive values;
- every committed artifact derived from the archive carries the recorded attribution and indicates the changes made.

### 2.4 Fetch and landing protocol

`scripts/fetch_her.py` MUST:

- use the pinned Zenodo record;
- download only explicitly selected files;
- record source URL, DOI, record version, access timestamp, filename, byte size, provided checksum, and locally calculated SHA-256;
- write a machine-readable `provenance.json`;
- retain downloaded bytes unchanged in an immutable raw landing prefix;
- fail on checksum mismatch;
- support a dry-run inventory mode;
- avoid downloading the large measurement video unless explicitly requested.

`scripts/inspect_her.py` MUST inspect the actual extracted files and produce a versioned inventory containing:

- archive paths;
- file formats;
- table names and columns;
- inferred and declared units;
- row and array dimensions;
- missing-value summaries;
- identifier ranges;
- duplicate checks;
- relationships among coordinates, libraries, LSVs, compositions, and fitted parameters.

Implementation code MUST NOT hardcode columns or internal paths before this inventory exists.

### 2.5 Raw, normalised, and derived layers

#### Raw layer

Byte-for-byte source archives and extracted source files. Never modified.

#### Normalised observation layer

A typed representation that:

- preserves source identifiers and file hashes;
- stores explicit units;
- records transformations such as delimiter parsing or column renaming;
- does not alter scientific values except explicit unit conversion;
- retains missing and unavailable measurements as explicit states.

#### Vendor-format parser evidence

A vendor parser is an interpretation layer, not a source replacement. The exact vendor file remains
the raw source artifact. Each attempted interpretation MUST retain a content-addressed parser record
that names the source bytes, explicit import profile, parser version, supported variant, decision,
and source locations used for accepted fields. Rejection MUST retain a stable error diagnostic and
MUST NOT create a partial normalised observation.

The Phase 4 Gamry boundary accepts only the documented Framework `7.07` CV `CURVE` layout pinned by
the redistributable synthetic fixture. `TITLE`, `NOTES`, and other non-table object types may be
preserved as uninterpreted labels; their contents assign no scientific meaning. The source string
`V vs. Ref.` remains distinct from a reference-electrode identity. A potential value may be stored
in volts while the reference scale stays `unknown`; no RHE, SHE, Ag/AgCl, correction, or compensation
state follows from the unit string.

Equivalent DTA and generic CSV inputs converge only after independent explicit profiles map their
source columns and units into the common CV roles. Equality of normalised values does not collapse
their source artifacts, parser records, transformation graphs, or provenance.

#### Galvanostatic electrolysis boundary

The electrolysis normalised layer contains recorded time, current or current density, and potential
series only after an explicit profile assigns every role and a role-compatible unit. Electrical
completeness means aligned series lengths, a strictly increasing time axis, and agreement with any
declared sampling interval; it is not evidence of product identity or quantity. Current sign
convention, area basis, cell geometry, reference scale, correction state, sampling, and interruptions
remain independently declared metadata states with controlled known values.
These profile-owned semantics cannot be supplemented, shadowed, or corrected on the existing
Experiment. A mapping, unit, quantity-kind, or profile-metadata correction creates a new immutable
profile and normalised observation while retaining the prior records.

Auxiliary analytical values remain source-linked user declarations. Each declaration names the exact
electrical source, retained analytical source bytes, sample and collection point, a declared method
and method version, and a source location. This boundary retains the source bytes but does not parse
that analytical location or claim that the declared value was extracted by LabBridge. LabBridge does
not ingest chromatography, assign products, or calculate conversion, selectivity, yield, or Faradaic
efficiency in this boundary. Adding such a derived quantity requires a separate versioned analysis
contract with dimensional checks and complete lineage.

#### Derived layer

Versioned metrics and analytical outputs. Source-provided fits and LabBridge-computed fits remain distinguishable.

A LabBridge re-fit MAY be implemented for validation, but the project MUST NOT claim reproduction until numerical tolerances, eligible records, exclusions, and method correspondence are documented and tested.

### 2.6 Replay semantics

The HER adapter receives a typed location candidate and returns:

- the actual recorded observation associated with that source location; or
- a structured unavailable or invalid outcome when no eligible source observation exists.

The adapter MUST NOT:

- interpolate a missing location;
- impute a failed or excluded measurement;
- substitute a GP-predicted property for an observed measurement;
- fabricate uncertainty;
- represent a replay as a newly performed experiment.

Repeated queries to the same source location return the same source observation identity. A campaign-level repeat may create a new attempt record, but not a fictitious independent measurement.

## 3. Synthetic environment — enzymatic-biosensor simulator

### 3.1 Purpose

The author’s original biosensor measurements are not publishable. The simulator therefore serves two bounded purposes:

1. **infrastructure testing:** deterministic generation of edge cases and failures that a fixed replay archive cannot provide;
2. **domain communication:** encode transparent, literature-supported hypotheses about how fabrication and operating parameters can affect electrochemical biosensor signals.

It is a **mechanistically informed phenomenological simulator**, not a validated digital twin, not a reconstruction of proprietary data, and not a source of new experimental evidence.

The full model and its limits are defined in `SIMULATOR_MODEL.md`.

### 3.2 Integrity requirements

Every synthetic record MUST include:

- `data_origin="synthetic"`;
- `execution_mode="simulation"`;
- `environment_id`;
- model version;
- canonical configuration hash;
- seed;
- component-model versions;
- explicit units;
- provenance to all generated parents.

Synthetic outputs MAY be exported and plotted. Every exported table, chart, filename, metadata block, and report section MUST identify them as synthetic.

### 3.3 Simulator decomposition

The simulator is composed of independent, testable layers:

- `SignalGenerator` — idealised concentration-to-current or potential-to-current response;
- `NoiseModel` — seeded measurement noise and baseline variation;
- `BatchEffectModel` — seeded between-batch offsets or scales;
- `FailureInjector` — transport, instrument-like, corruption, timeout, and collision scenarios;
- `AnalysisPipeline` — derives declared metrics from generated signals.

Failure injection MUST NOT be hidden inside the scientific signal equations. A “bad scientific outcome” and a runtime or instrument failure remain distinguishable.

### 3.4 Candidate configuration

The candidate configuration includes typed values for:

- carbon loading;
- surfactant identity and concentration;
- membrane presence, material class, and thickness proxy;
- two-electrode or three-electrode setup;
- reference geometry or placement proxy;
- cleaning protocol category;
- immersion time;
- analyte concentration schedule;
- interferent schedule;
- batch identifier;
- environment temperature if modelled.

Each parameter has declared units, allowed ranges, and a documented domain of validity.

### 3.5 Readouts and operational definitions

The simulator MAY produce:

- sensitivity;
- limit of detection;
- linear range;
- selectivity;
- durability or signal retention.

Each metric MUST have one implemented operational definition. Examples:

- **sensitivity:** slope of a declared calibration interval, with fit method and units;
- **LOD:** a documented blank-based or regression-based rule such as `k × sigma_blank / slope`, including the chosen `k` and blank-replicate assumptions;
- **linear range:** the largest declared interval satisfying a specified fit or deviation criterion;
- **selectivity:** response ratio or interference error under a declared interferent protocol;
- **durability:** retained sensitivity or current response after a declared immersion duration relative to baseline.

The project MUST NOT mix several LOD definitions without labelling them.

### 3.6 Parameter-effect hypotheses

Parameter effects are hypotheses under declared assumptions, not universal monotonic truths.

| Parameter | Model hypothesis under declared conditions | Possible counter-effects or limits |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon loading | Increased electroactive area may increase signal magnitude within a useful range | Excess loading may increase capacitive current, transport limitations, aggregation, or noise; the relation may saturate or reverse |
| Surfactant | Wetting and enzyme immobilisation may change sensitivity and retention | Effect depends on surfactant identity, concentration, enzyme stability, electrode material, and membrane formulation |
| Membrane | A diffusion barrier may extend an apparent linear range or reduce interferent transport | It may also reduce response time and sensitivity; selectivity depends on membrane chemistry and interferent properties |
| Two- vs three-electrode setup | Reference and counter-electrode configuration may affect potential control, bias, or drift | The magnitude is device- and geometry-dependent; a two-electrode arrangement is not universally noisier under every condition |
| Reference geometry | Geometry may influence uncompensated resistance and apparent potential | Effect depends on electrolyte conductivity, current, distance, cell geometry, and compensation |
| Cleaning protocol | Cleaning may alter fouling, baseline stability, and reproducibility | Aggressive cleaning may damage films, enzymes, mediators, or membranes |
| Immersion time | Hydration, swelling, leaching, fouling, or enzyme loss may shift response over time | Short-time conditioning can improve stability before longer-term degradation; behaviour may be non-monotonic |

Each implemented relationship MUST include a literature citation, equation or rule, configured magnitude, valid parameter range, and qualitative test. Unsupported magnitudes remain explicitly synthetic choices.

## 4. Failure and anomaly data

LabBridge distinguishes:

- **execution failures:** timeout, worker death, transport error, empty response;
- **data corruption:** clipping, spikes, malformed arrays, missing units, impossible axis ordering;
- **scientific-quality failures:** failed validation, unstable fit, insufficient blank replicates;
- **unfavourable scientific results:** valid data showing poor performance.

Only the first three are failures in the runtime sense. A poor but valid catalyst or biosensor result is a successful observation.

All required scenarios are catalogued in `FAILURE_MATRIX.md`.

## 5. Content addressing and canonical representation

An observation hash MUST incorporate:

- canonical array bytes;
- dtype and byte order;
- shape;
- quantity names and units;
- ordered axes;
- schema version;
- relevant source identifiers;
- normalisation version when values were transformed.

A candidate hash MUST incorporate:

- candidate schema version;
- all typed parameter values in canonical units;
- explicit nulls;
- environment ID.

The project MUST define how decimals, NaNs, infinities, missing values, string normalisation, and mapping order are serialised before hashing.

---

## 6. Lineage and provenance closure

Every accepted derived metric MUST resolve to one of two roots:

### Observed root

- exact Zenodo record and version;
- source filename and checksum;
- internal source path or record identifier;
- raw observation object hash;
- parsing and analysis versions.

### Synthetic root

- simulator model version;
- canonical configuration hash;
- seed;
- component-model versions;
- generated observation hash;
- analysis version.

Lineage tests MUST fail when a metric has a missing parent, unknown version, or ambiguous origin.

---

## 7. Correction and invalidation policy

- raw source bytes are never rewritten;
- parsing corrections create a new normalised observation version;
- scientific invalidation creates an explicit relation and reason;
- re-analysis creates new derived metrics with a new analysis version;
- current views may exclude invalidated records;
- historical evidence bundles retain the state that was released at that time;
- a corrected public result is a new bundle, not a mutation of the old bundle.

---

## 8. Repository and redistribution policy

### Committed

- independently generated schema-compatible fixture data;
- small synthetic biosensor examples;
- schemas, manifests, and provenance examples;
- public evidence bundles generated entirely from redistributable inputs;
- source inventory metadata when redistribution is permitted.

### Git-ignored

- full downloaded HER archives;
- extracted archive data;
- large generated simulator outputs;
- local PostgreSQL and MinIO volumes;
- secrets and deployment credentials.

### Permitted by ADR-009, subject to attribution

Redistribution of HER rows, transformed subsets, archive-derived fixtures, and archive-derived plots is permitted under CC BY 4.0 with attribution and an indication of changes. Anything committed under this allowance MUST carry that attribution on the artifact itself, not only in a commit message.

The git-ignore policy above still applies to the bulk archive and extracted data: it keeps the DOI as the single source of truth and the repository small. It is not a licence restriction, and it does not forbid a small attributed excerpt where one is genuinely needed.

### Forbidden regardless of licence

- presenting a source-provided fit as independently reproduced without a validation artifact;
- presenting GP-predicted values as measured;
- redistributing any material the record does not itself license.

---

## 9. Scientific assumptions requiring support

Before the simulator is promoted from `planned` to `implemented`, the project MUST provide sources for:

- the chosen idealised enzymatic response model;
- the selected LOD definition;
- diffusion-barrier effects of the membrane representation;
- plausible noise and drift structures;
- any effect attributed to surfactant, carbon loading, cleaning, or immersion;
- any relation between reference geometry and apparent potential;
- durability-decay or conditioning dynamics;
- acceptable parameter ranges and units.

The project may cite primary literature, standards, or authoritative textbooks. A citation supports a qualitative mechanism; it does not automatically validate an arbitrary numerical coefficient.

---

## 10. Future work

The following are outside V1:

- a matched simulation-versus-experiment environment for genuine multi-fidelity optimisation;
- computed versus experimental band-gap matching;
- additional catalyst families;
- live instrument integration;
- empirical calibration of the biosensor simulator against publishable data.

Future work MUST satisfy the same provenance and licence gates before entering the core system.
